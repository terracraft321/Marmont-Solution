# Reproducing the final solution

The frozen inputs define a proposed edition of the 24-row cipher body. The program joins one value per token from inputs/key.tsv to reconstruct solution/literal.txt. Values are applied consistently wherever a label occurs. Empty and punctuation values are preserved; TARGET remains ⟦UNRESOLVED:TARGET⟧. No model service is called during reproduction.

The edition has 1,310 units: 1,300 cipher units and ten visible cleartext letters spelling CONSEQUENT. There are 155 cipher labels and eight cleartext label types. Fifty cipher labels occur once.

The editorial French and English are supplied interpretations. The French has 29 documented character-intervention blocks after accent, punctuation, and spacing normalization. inputs/editorial_interventions.tsv uses one-based character offsets with exclusive ends. In this coordinate system the unresolved TARGET marker occupies one character, ¤. The verifier applies every recorded intervention to the normalized literal rows and checks the result against inputs/reading_rows.json. It separately verifies that the eleven composed French paragraphs match those rows under normalization. English translation is supplied text; it is not claimed to be independently generated by the decoder.

These categories are not accuracy percentages or probabilities. inputs/uncertainties.tsv retains every B label. Six have explicit competing proposals: TARGET, FOUR_DOTS, RHO, LOW_INWARD_ARCH, ARCH_COMPOUND, and SLOOP. The other B assignments remain contextual or have an uncertain empty/punctuation function. Candidate lists are not an exhaustive historical vocabulary. inputs/defects.json records 17 final defect sites, including four gaps and 13 existing-unit sites covering 14 C units.

The 33 partial-table agreements cover 435 cipher units in total: 427 A units and eight C units. They are comparisons to the reported modern ARCSI/Tant table, not proof that the complete historical codebook has survived or that those mappings were discovered independently.

**Image reconstruction.** sources/cipher_plate.png is converted to grayscale without resizing. inputs/units.tsv contains the final label and integer rectangle for every unit. Coordinates use a top-left origin with left/top inclusive and right/bottom exclusive. Row crops add small fixed margins around the union of their unit rectangles. Overhead and adjacent ink can overlap a crop; the complete plate remains available for inspection.

The glyph selections use source rectangles and explicit corner exclusions in inputs/glyph_selection.json. A single global threshold of 225 produces black pixels with alpha 255 at or below the threshold, and alpha zero otherwise. Each glyph has exactly three transparent pixels of padding. Raw companions retain grayscale source pixels plus white padding. Wider source contexts preserve neighbouring strokes. Current faintness, blot, and boundary limitations remain in the glyph table. The contact sheet places each glyph at native pixel scale. PNG compression bytes can depend on the image library; verification compares the actual pixels, dimensions, and masks against the source rules.

**Verification.** The default verifier checks the complete distributed-file manifest, final key frequencies, unit labels and coordinates, evidence-category coverage, all editorial interventions, the generated text files, every glyph/companion/context and unit/row crop, and local links in START_HERE.html. It reports failure with a nonzero exit code on any failed check. --text-only explicitly omits pixel checks and does not represent a complete image verification.

The successful computation establishes reproducibility and consistency of this proposed solution. It does not authenticate the original manuscript, establish the exact historical date or sender, prove the reading unique, or establish first-ever decipherment. The historical comparison supports a March 1809 interpretation; the literal text and the remaining uncertainty are available for independent inspection.
